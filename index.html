I am a PhD student at EPFL and Idiap Research Institute, and a MATS Scholar, with close ties to the Institute of Philosophy at the University of Bern.

I study understanding, reasoning, and world modeling in language models. I do mechanistic interpretability with philosophy, investigating how models implement these capacities with insights from my philosophical background. I also do philosophy with mechanistic interpretability, drawing on empirical findings to argue about whether, and in what sense, models can be said to have these capacities.

Mechanistic Interpretability

World Modeling in Transformers
Pierre Beckmann, Matthieu Queloz, André Freitas
arXiv preprint
arXiv / interactive explorer / code / thread

Vafa et al. found that a GPT model trained on taxi rides through Manhattan still behaved in a way that implied an incoherent map. We look inside the model and find a faithful internal map and a goal compass. What sometimes leads the model astray is that it stores the map in superposition, like a taxi driver who runs out of space on the page when drawing a map.

Probing Persona-Dependent Preferences in Language Models
Oscar Gilg, Pierre Beckmann, Daniel Paleka, Patrick Butlin
NeurIPS, 2026
arXiv

Using linear probes on residual-stream activations, we find a preference vector that tracks a model's preferences across prompts and situations — and show this representation is largely shared across personas, predicting and steering choices even for an adversarial persona.

Why We Care About Understanding: Competence through Predictive Compression
Matthieu Queloz, Pierre Beckmann
PhilPapers preprint
paper

By reverse-engineering the function of both the state and the concept of understanding, we develop a compression account: understanding arises from pressures to build predictive models that are accurate yet compressed enough to store, demonstrate, and transmit.

Reasoning in LLMs

SciR: A Controllable Benchmark for Scientific Reasoning in LLMs
Pierre Beckmann, Marco Valentino, André Freitas
NeurIPS, 2026
paper / code / data

A multi-paradigm benchmark for scientific reasoning — deduction, induction, and causal abduction — where tasks are generated as formal objects with verifiable answers, then rendered into scientific documents. Two difficulty axes, inference complexity and premise obfuscation, can be varied independently to decompose model failure into extraction and inference.

Adaptive LLM-Symbolic Reasoning via Dynamic Logical Solver Composition

EACL, 2026
paper
